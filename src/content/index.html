Off the CV

What a job history leaves out: how I learn, how I work, and what keeps me going.

How I learn

Most of my tools were never taught to me.

VBA, Qlik, Alteryx, Power BI and Python each arrived because the work needed them, and I learned them on the job. The certificates came later, to fill the gaps. The latest additions are databases, web security and payments.

How I work

The polishing happens in my own time.

Deadlines are kept. If a report could be cleaner, a check tighter or a process faster, I fix it after hours rather than hold the work back. People get the finished version, on time.

How I think

Every figure should trace back to its source.

That's the actuary in me. Give me a system and I want to know how it fits together: where the data comes from, where it breaks, and how to make it easier for the people who rely on it.

How I move

2026 started with a leap.

I left a steady role to grow: a job search in the UAE, a move from Karachi to Lahore, and freelance work. The evenings went into learning how AI is changing data work. One of those evening projects is now an app in open beta.

Who keeps me honest

My toughest reviewer is at home.

Bismah Khalid's notebook started FINSTRIDE, and her feedback shapes every screen. She tests everything before anyone else sees it.
